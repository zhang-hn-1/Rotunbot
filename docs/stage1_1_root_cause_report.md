# Stage 1.1 V49 Root-Cause Audit

## 1. Baseline reproduction

The audit was run from `codex/stage1-1-root-cause-audit`, based on
`codex/stage1-v49-waypoint-switching@60be1d8`.  The frozen checkpoint was
`model_300.pt`, SHA256
`5cd24ff2b8b4b0a16e7a96f0bdd707c25d27c08c9ed7ae8931c37103bba1769a`.
The same checkpoint, seed, trajectory order, episode IDs, and generated
initial poses were used for all v49_reference AB runs.  Each arm contains ten
A and ten B episodes; the route success definition remains
`route_complete / episodes`.

Baseline reproduced the failure trend: 9/20 routes completed (45%), with
32/60 waypoints reached.  There were zero intermediate resets, skips, or
NaN/Inf episodes.  The baseline split was A 6/10 and B 3/10.

## 2. Trajectory A vs B

| Run | A success | B success | Overall SR | Waypoint reach |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Baseline | 6/10 | 3/10 | 9/20 (45%) | 32/60 (53.3%) |
| Smooth=True | 5/10 | 3/10 | 8/20 (40%) | 29/60 (48.3%) |
| Rolling floor 0.10 | 7/10 | 5/10 | 12/20 (60%) | 38/60 (63.3%) |
| Combined | 7/10 | 4/10 | 11/20 (55%) | 37/60 (61.7%) |

Trajectory B is consistently weaker than A in baseline and rolling-floor
conditions.  Its curved middle waypoint exposes the same low-speed and
transition limitations more often; it is not evidence of a waypoint skip or
reset bug.

## 3. Initial yaw dependence

Baseline route SR by sampled initial yaw was: -15° 0/2, -10° 1/2, -5° 5/8,
5° 1/2, 10° 0/2, and 15° 2/4.  The two extreme failures at -15° and 10° are
consistent with heading dependence, but the sample is small and the yaw
distribution is generated by the fixed seed.  The yaw breakdown is retained
in every `summary.json` for follow-up with a larger controlled sample.

## 4. Smooth reference AB

The only change was `command_reference_is_smooth=True`.  Smooth=True produced
8/20 versus baseline 9/20 routes and 29/60 versus 32/60 waypoints.  Angular
tracking MAE changed from 0.018157 to 0.017175 rad/s and P95 absolute error
from 0.046904 to 0.040431 rad/s, but this modest tracking change did not
translate to route improvement.

The rate-feedforward active ratio was 0.000000 in both baseline and Smooth=True
runs.  Therefore this audit did not observe the required “baseline off,
smooth on” contrast.  H1 is not supported by this release configuration;
`smooth_control_disabled_detected` is consequently not asserted.

## 5. Rolling-floor AB

The diagnostic floor was `0.10 m/s` on nonterminal route commands only, after
which the unchanged V49 static projection was applied.  Rolling floor improved
route SR from 45% to 60% and waypoint reach from 53.3% to 63.3%.  Failure
episodes entering `|v|<0.10` fell from 6 to 3, episodes entering `|v|<0.08`
fell from 6 to 3, and detected low-speed yaw-collapse episodes fell from 13 to
6.

This is a diagnostic causal result, not a proposal to make `v >= 0.10 m/s` a
formal controller rule.

## 6. Combined AB

Combined (`smooth=True` plus rolling floor) produced 11/20 routes and 37/60
waypoints.  It was weaker than rolling-only (12/20 and 38/60), so there is no
evidence of a useful additive effect.  Its rate-feedforward ratio was only
0.000577, still far from a clearly active smooth-reference regime.

## 7. Command-transition distribution

Transitions are computed between adjacent 5 Hz projected commands, not from
50 Hz action samples.  Baseline values were:

| Metric | Baseline | Smooth=True | Rolling floor |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| P95 `|delta_v|` (m/s) | 0.003892 | 0.003843 | 0.003949 |
| P95 `|delta_w|` (rad/s) | 0.004063 | 0.003967 | 0.008075 |
| Fraction `|delta_v| > 0.008` | 0.00974 | 0.00855 | 0.01132 |
| Fraction `|delta_w| > 0.004` | 0.05105 | 0.05000 | 0.08760 |
| Fraction `|delta_v| > 0.016` | 0.00974 | 0.00855 | 0.01132 |
| Fraction `|delta_w| > 0.008` | 0.01053 | 0.00829 | 0.05066 |

Baseline maxima were 0.08665 m/s and 0.03510 rad/s, substantially above the
0.008 m/s and 0.004 rad/s random-walk increments.  Rolling floor itself
creates additional large transitions when the floor engages.  One meaningful
yaw sign reversal was observed in the rolling and combined populations; the
per-event measured speeds and deltas are stored in the episode summaries.

## 8. Low-speed yaw behavior

Baseline entered the low-speed region in six failing episodes at both reported
thresholds, and 13 episodes met the diagnostic collapse rule: at least 0.4 s
below 0.08 m/s with at least 10° bearing error and less than 20% bearing
reduction.  Rolling-only reduced these counts to 3, 3, and 6 respectively.
The mean bearing reduction after E2 was 0.0960 rad for baseline and 0.0827 rad
for rolling-only; this aggregate is affected by which episodes reach E2, so the
collapse count and per-episode traces are the stronger evidence.

The audit records static feasibility separately from dynamic transition
severity.  In baseline, 3055 of 3820 route ticks were clipped by the static
projection (`static_feasible=False`); this is an observed interface/load
characteristic, not a new constraint.

## 9. Contact-yaw-damping behavior

Contact yaw-damping buffers were read only.  The maximum absolute recorded
damping torque was 0.3600 in baseline, 0.5000 in Smooth=True, 0.3389 in
rolling-only, and 0.4046 in the maze rolling replicate.  The results show
that damping is present in the low-speed traces, but the AB does not isolate a
damping-parameter causal effect.  No contact-damping parameter was modified.

## 10. Terminal settling analysis

Terminal settling was counted only after `route_complete=True`; incomplete
routes contribute only `last_measured_v/w` and never count as terminal settling
failures.

| Run | Route complete | Safe terminal speed | True terminal speed failure | Mean stop distance | P95 stop distance |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Baseline | 9 | 8 | 1 | 0.0875 m | 0.1799 m |
| Smooth=True | 8 | 6 | 2 | 0.0682 m | 0.1395 m |
| Rolling floor | 12 | 9 | 3 | 0.1507 m | 0.2254 m |
| Combined | 11 | 9 | 2 | 0.1393 m | 0.1821 m |

The settling CSVs contain 0.1 s samples of distance, measured velocity,
measured yaw rate, and actuator state, including time to `|v|<=0.10` and
`|v|<=0.05`.  Baseline has too few true terminal failures to distinguish a
high-level stopping-distance mismatch from an intrinsic braking tail.

## 11. Root-cause ranking

| Hypothesis | Ranking | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| H1: smooth=False disables relevant V49 dynamic compensation | NOT SUPPORTED | Smooth=True did not activate rate-FF and did not improve SR. |
| H2: low-speed `<0.08 m/s` causes loss of yaw correction | STRONGLY SUPPORTED | Rolling floor improved SR by 15 pp and reduced collapse detections 13→6. |
| H3: waypoint transitions exceed V49 training distribution | STRONGLY SUPPORTED | Baseline maxima were about 10.8× and 8.8× the nominal training increments. |
| H4: static feasible set is insufficient for dynamic reachability | INCONCLUSIVE | Static clipping and transition severity are observed, but no explicit dynamic reachable-set model was fitted. |
| H5: terminal failure is mainly high-level stopping-distance mismatch | WEAKLY SUPPORTED | One of nine completed baseline routes failed terminal speed; stop distance is variable. |
| H6: terminal failure is mainly intrinsic V49 braking tail | INCONCLUSIVE | Settling traces expose the tail, but the sample does not isolate its cause. |
| H7: URDF transfer mismatch is a primary cause | NOT SUPPORTED | Strongest mechanism had the same 12/20 SR on `v49_reference` and `Rotunbot.urdf`. |

## 12. Recommendation

The primary root cause is low-speed dynamic yaw reachability, amplified by
large waypoint command transitions.  The secondary issue is the mismatch
between waypoint switching dynamics and the V49 random-walk transition
distribution.  The smooth-reference hypothesis was not demonstrated by this
release because rate-FF stayed inactive in both arms.

V49 retraining: **NO** based on this audit.  Do not promote the rolling floor
or Smooth=True flag into the formal Stage 1 controller without defining the
navigation command contract and repeating a controlled evaluation.

Stage 2 recommendation: **YES, design a Dynamic Reference Governor** that
preserves the existing V49 projection and explicitly handles low-speed
rolling/yaw reachability.  Its design should also define whether
waypoint-generated commands qualify as a smooth reference.  This audit does
not implement that governor.

The strongest mechanism was replicated on `maze / Rotunbot.urdf` with rolling
floor only: A=7/10, B=5/10, 12/20 routes, and 38/60 waypoints.  Low-speed
collapse detections were 5, consistent with the v49_reference direction.
